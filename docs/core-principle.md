# Urge Principles

## What is Urge?

**Urge is a founder transformation platform for first-time entrepreneurs, builders, and career changers.**

Urge is not a course. It is not an LMS. It is not a collection of lessons about entrepreneurship.

It is designed to help people **become capable of starting** by changing what they do, not simply by increasing what they know.

The measure of progress is not:

> How much did someone learn?

It is:

> **What can this person now do that they couldn't do before?**

---

## The Problem We Are Solving

People who want to start something often get stuck before they ever begin.

There are three traps we want Urge to help people escape.

### 1. The Knowledge Trap

People consume books, videos, courses, frameworks and advice because learning feels like progress.

But learning can also become a very comfortable way of avoiding the uncomfortable part:

**starting.**

Urge does not reward consumption.

It moves people toward experience, action, reflection and evidence.

---

### 2. The Silicon Valley Trap

Much of the popular startup world assumes a particular kind of business:

- technology
- venture capital
- rapid growth
- large teams
- fundraising
- pitch decks
- complex startup language

That is not the definition of entrepreneurship.

Someone can start a business with a skill, a problem they understand, a few people they know, a small amount of money, and a willingness to try.

Urge is for that person too.

We do not assume that someone wants to build a venture-backed technology company.

We help people discover what **their** business could be.

---

### 3. The Build-Too-Soon Trap

People often jump from:

> "I have an idea."

straight to:

> "I need to build something."

They create software, buy inventory, make a website, design a logo or spend money before understanding the problem, the people involved, or whether anyone actually wants what they are making.

Urge slows this down.

Not to create more planning.

To create **better evidence**.

---

# The Urge Approach

## Transformation Before Instruction

Urge is not primarily concerned with teaching people everything they need to know.

It is concerned with helping them experience things that change what they believe they are capable of doing.

The sequence is:

**Experience → Reflection → Evidence → Changed behaviour**

Not:

**Content → Quiz → Completion**

A person should leave an experience having:

- noticed something
- tried something
- learned something from what actually happened
- made a decision
- taken an action
- or changed what they intend to do next

---

# Move Before You Are Ready

Confidence does not have to come first.

People often wait to feel:

- confident
- certain
- prepared
- knowledgeable
- qualified

before they act.

Urge reverses this.

**Action comes before confidence.**

We create small opportunities for people to act while uncertainty is still present.

Then we help them look at what actually happened.

The goal is not to convince someone that everything will work out.

The goal is to help them discover:

> **I can act even when I don't know how this will turn out.**

---

# Start With What You Already Have

Urge draws on an effectual approach to entrepreneurship.

Instead of beginning with:

> "What business should you build?"

we begin with:

- Who are you?
- What do you know?
- What can you do?
- Who do you know?
- What have you already experienced?
- What can you afford to risk?
- What is already within reach?

The starting point is not an imaginary future.

It is **what is already in your hands.**

---

# The Ask

Starting something usually involves other people.

A customer.

A friend.

A supplier.

A potential partner.

Someone who knows something you don't.

Someone who might open a door.

Urge helps people get comfortable asking.

Not by teaching networking.

By actually asking.

The person makes the ask, leaves the app, experiences what happens, and comes back to reflect on it.

The experience matters more than the lesson about the experience.

---

# Real World Before Theoretical

Whenever possible, Urge should move people out of the app and into the real world.

If there is something to find out, **go find out.**

If there is someone to talk to, **talk to them.**

If there is something to ask, **ask.**

If there is something to try, **try it.**

Then come back.

Urge helps the person make sense of what happened.

The world provides the evidence.

---

# AI Is A Mirror, Not An Oracle

AI should not tell people who they are.

It should not diagnose them.

It should not pretend to know their hidden motivations.

It should not generate a business plan and hand it to them.

AI can help someone notice things they may have missed.

It can say:

> "One thing I notice..."

It can point out a contradiction.

It can ask a useful follow-up question.

It can reflect something the person has said back to them.

But the person decides what it means.

**AI can help someone see.  
The person decides what they see.**

---

# The Narrative Spine

Every meaningful Urge experience should have a reason for happening.

The experience follows a simple narrative:

**Situation → Complication → Investigation → Reveal → Decision**

### Situation

What is true right now?

### Complication

What is getting in the way?

What makes this harder than it first appears?

### Investigation

Let's look more closely.

Ask questions.

Try something.

Gather evidence.

Talk to someone.

Notice what happens.

### Reveal

What did we actually discover?

This is not a motivational speech.

It is a moment to pause and look honestly at what has emerged.

### Decision

Now what?

The decision might be:

- take an action
- make a commitment
- create a task
- ask someone
- try something
- change direction
- continue
- stop
- or consciously decide not to act yet

The important thing is that the person **does something with what they discovered.**

---

# One Human Question At A Time

Urge should feel like a conversation, not an interrogation.

Ask one meaningful question.

Listen.

Respond to what the person actually said.

Then continue.

Do not present a wall of questions simply because the database needs more fields.

The experience should feel natural even when the system is collecting structured information underneath.

---

# Use Their Words

When someone says:

> "I keep thinking about starting a photography business, but I don't know if I'm good enough."

Urge should continue from **that sentence**.

Not:

> "Let's assess your entrepreneurial confidence."

The person's own words are often more useful than our categories.

Structured data can exist underneath.

The conversation should remain human.

---

# Don't Manufacture Positivity

Not everything needs to become a win.

Someone can discover:

> "I don't actually want this."

Someone can get rejected.

Someone can realise they don't have enough time.

Someone can decide:

> "Not now."

That is allowed.

Urge should not respond to every outcome with:

> "Amazing!"

or

> "That's great!"

Sometimes the right response is:

> "Okay. That didn't go the way you expected. What do you notice?"

Truth is more valuable than forced positivity.

---

# Don't Sound Like A Coach

Urge should feel like a thoughtful friend sitting beside you.

Not:

- a life coach
- a motivational speaker
- a management consultant
- a startup guru
- a productivity app
- a Silicon Valley founder

The tone should be:

- warm
- curious
- direct
- calm
- honest
- occasionally playful
- comfortable with silence and uncertainty

Prefer:

> "Okay. Let's slow this down for a second."

> "What is actually going on?"

> "Why haven't you?"

> "And yet, you're still here."

> "What happened?"

> "What do you notice?"

> "Okay. Now what do you want to do with that?"

Avoid:

> "Unlock your entrepreneurial potential."

> "Let's leverage your network."

> "You are crushing it!"

> "Here are five steps to success."

> "High-performing entrepreneurs understand..."

> "You need to..."

---

# The App Should Not Feel Like A Course

The underlying program has missions, quests, nodes, progress and completion.

The person using Urge should not need to think about any of that.

Those structures exist to help us create a coherent experience.

They are not the experience itself.

The user should feel:

> "Urge is helping me figure something out."

Not:

> "I have three more activities to complete."

---

# The Transformation Is The Product

Every experience should ultimately answer:

> **What is different about this person because they went through this?**

Not:

> What did they read?

Not:

> What did they complete?

Not:

> What information did we give them?

But:

> **What did they experience?**

> **What did they discover?**

> **What did they do?**

> **What evidence did they get?**

> **What changed?**

That is what Urge is building.

---

# A Simple Test For Everything We Build

Before adding a screen, question, piece of content, AI interaction, exercise or feature, ask:

### Does this help the person...

**See something?**

**Feel something?**

**Try something?**

**Learn something from reality?**

**Make a decision?**

**Take action?**

**Change their behaviour?**

If not, ask:

> **Why is this here?**

If the answer is only:

> "Because courses normally have this."

We probably don't need it.

---

# The Standard

Urge should leave people with less theory and more evidence.

Less waiting and more movement.

Less fear of asking and more asking.

Less pretending to know and more finding out.

Less planning as avoidance and more action.

And eventually:

> **Someone who once said "I want to start something" can look back and say "I started."**
